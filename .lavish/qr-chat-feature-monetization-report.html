Keep the social experience free. Charge venues for running a better community. Add a consumer subscription when people return often enough to value it.

Product strategy / Discussion draft / 19 September 2026

Recommendation: venue revenue firstPrices are test hypothesesWeb + iOS + Android

Working assumption: a social connection product for real venues and events, starting in one city. Euro examples are for comparison; business domicile, launch country, audience age and tax treatment are not yet established. This is a proposal, not an approved feature specification.

Business model options

Start here

Venues fund the network

Sell useful host tools to coworking spaces, social cafés and recurring event organizers. One customer brings an entire room of potential users.

Tradeoff: venues need proof of repeat engagement. Sales, onboarding and host participation remain real work.

Second revenue stream

Regulars buy extra value

Sell personalization, organization and metered convenience to people who already use QR Chat repeatedly.

Tradeoff: occasional visits are a weak reason to maintain a monthly subscription. Cosmetics alone may not be enough.

Later experiment

Events and sponsorships

Offer an organizer event pass, then clearly labeled local sponsorships when discovery has real traffic.

Tradeoff: one-off revenue is less predictable. Advertising adds clutter and sales overhead before there is an audience.

The main risk is an empty room. Ten people participating together in one venue are more useful than hundreds of signups scattered across places and times. Prioritize concentrated activity before monetization experiments.

Build on the real product.

Repository documentation and shared API were inspected. No live backend or customer data was accessed.

Current product contract

  • Authenticated QR joining, profiles, group messages, friendships and direct messages.
  • One active group per person; membership expires after 24 hours. Joining another replaces the previous membership.
  • The last membership leaving removes the room and its messages. Friendships and DMs survive venue membership; unfriending deletes DM history.
  • Interactive mobile web, iOS and Android; desktop is reserved for marketing and QR handoff.

Proposed additions

  • Server-backed blocking, reporting and moderation are launch requirements and are currently missing from the documented schema.
  • Notifications, venue ownership, events, discovery, saved places, media and every paid plan below are new work.
  • Paid access must not quietly extend group retention, restore deleted chats or bypass friendship acceptance.
  • A venue administration portal would be a separately approved surface; the consumer desktop site stays marketing-only.

Good upgrade moments

A regular chooses a premium theme, creates another advanced collection or exhausts a translation allowance. A host schedules their next event after seeing the first event's results.

The upgrade explains the selected benefit, price, renewal and cancellation. It has an obvious free exit.

Weak paywalls

Charging to read a received message, accept a friend, block harassment, join the first room or keep an existing friend. These put friction directly into the network.

Avoid premium access to expired groups, paid safety badges and selling access to people who have not consented.

What could be added?

Filter by who benefits. All cards describe proposals; priority is based on trust, conversation quality and recurring value.

14 proposed features

Launch gate / Free

Reporting, blocking and moderation

Report a message or person, block future contact and enforce decisions on the server. Add rate limits, review workflow, appeals and a support contact. The policy must specify what blocked users can see in a shared room.

Launch gate / Trust

Verified venue ownership

Distinguish the actual venue operator from a person who copied a QR. Let a verified operator set the welcome and report misuse. A reusable QR proves knowledge of a code, not physical presence; do not claim everyone is currently on site.

Next / Free

A helpful first conversation

Add replies, reactions and a host-written prompt such as “Anyone here for the language exchange?” Show genuine activity and an honest quiet-room state. Never invent users, posts or crowd counts.

Next / Free

Useful notifications

Prioritize accepted-friend DMs and friend requests, with opt-in controls, quiet hours and sensible bundling. Ask for notification permission after a relevant action. Measure useful returns and mute rates, not notification volume.

Next / Free

Conversation intent

Optional interests and “open to meeting people” preferences give people a reason to talk. Keep basic profile information and contact controls free. Do not make disclosure compulsory or imply a person's availability from payment.

Pilot / Shared value

Event agenda and RSVP

A host lists a trivia night, language exchange or coworking meetup. Participants can RSVP, view the agenda and answer polls. RSVPing does not grant remote room membership; it is a separate event record.

Pilot / Venue

Host operations

Branded printable QR signs, welcome templates, scheduled activities and staff permissions. Sell organization and repeatability. Essential abuse response remains available regardless of a venue's subscription.

Pilot / Venue

Evidence of repeat engagement

Show aggregate joins, participation and repeat visits. Separate messages from unique participants. Suppress small audience breakdowns and never expose DM contents or sell identifiable visit histories. Do not claim that engagement proves incremental sales.

After retention / Free + Plus

Saved places and collections

Let everyone bookmark venues; offer advanced organization to regulars. Saved places do not retain old room messages or bypass a fresh join. Make browsing venue information distinct from joining its room.

After retention / Plus

Personalization and organization

Profile themes, tasteful supporter markers, custom app icons and friend folders. Preserve accessible default themes for everyone. Do not give paying users automatic prominence over other people.

After retention / Metered

On-demand translation

Useful for tourists, hostels and multilingual meetups. Start with a free sample and a clearly capped paid allowance sized against actual costs. Explain external processing before sending message content; retain normal deletion behavior.

Explore later / Discovery

Nearby venues and plans

List participating venues and upcoming events using coarse location and optional filters. Avoid a live map of individuals. Only build once enough real venues make the list useful; keep joining rules separate from discovery.

Explore later / Revenue

Loyalty and partner offers

Hosts can fund a real benefit for returning participants, with explicit opt-in and fraud controls. Test redemptions before promising “exclusive deals” in Plus. A digital subscription and a physical purchase need distinct checkout treatment.

Separate product decision

Persistent communities and media

Opt-in clubs could support recurring relationships; photos and voice notes could enrich them. Multiple active rooms or longer history change today's lifecycle. Persistent groups, storage, moderation and consent need an approved product and data proposal first.

Practical conversion tactics

  • Value before the ask: show a useful host report before offering next month's plan.
  • Try a real benefit: one premium theme preview or a small translation allowance; no blocking overlay on a first scan.
  • Annual after trust: show the full annual charge and the real monthly comparison, then test whether renewals justify the discount.
  • Relevant referrals: reward a completed first event or retained venue, not a raw signup. Cap rewards to discourage farming.
  • Recover failed payments: use provider retries, clear notices and a defined grace period before removing premium access.

A distribution plan that fits

  • Choose one segment with recurring social intent: language exchanges, coworking communities or hostel events.
  • Recruit 5-10 hosts in one city, each with a named person responsible for welcoming participants.
  • Place QR signs where people wait or gather. Explain the immediate benefit in one sentence.
  • Start with a weekly hosted occasion to concentrate participation in time.
  • Use mobile web for the first interaction; invite native installation after a valuable exchange.
Do not copy fake scarcity or forced friction. Avoid fabricated likes, fake crowd counts, resettable countdowns, hidden renewals, cancellation mazes and unlimited AI promises. Sell a benefit that remains useful after the first purchase.

Use hosted Stripe Checkout, Billing and the customer portal for a web-only venue administration product. Keep its organization billing separate from individual Plus subscriptions.

When Plus launches, use Apple and Google native purchases through RevenueCat, with Stripe web purchases connected to the same consumer entitlement.

RevenueCat's own Billing engine currently excludes B2B. Its separate Stripe Billing integration is a different product path. Integration options.

When I would choose Paddle

If global consumer tax administration is a bigger concern than checkout flexibility, compare Paddle + RevenueCat against Stripe + RevenueCat and Stripe Managed Payments.

At €4.99, the example standard Stripe processing + Billing fees total about €0.36 before tax tools, RevenueCat and other costs. This is arithmetic from the cited fee schedule, not a quote. Compare complete costs and responsibilities, not just one percentage.

Native checkout rules that affect this proposal

Use Apple In-App Purchase and Google Play Billing as the default for native digital subscriptions. Apple permits external purchase calls to action in the US storefront; other storefronts and Google's alternative-payment programs have specific conditions. Do not show the same web checkout link everywhere. QR codes are room-entry links, not a workaround for paid digital unlocks. Apple rules · Google rules.

Apple's Small Business Program offers a 15% commission to qualifying, enrolled developers. Google lists 15% for auto-renewing subscriptions. RevenueCat fees are additional; taxes and regional programs affect actual proceeds. Apple program · Google service fees.

A web venue portal avoids selling host features inside the consumer app initially. Selling host tools inside a native app needs a separate store-policy review. Real-world event tickets or purchases are a separate product category from digital host tools.

What a robust implementation would need
  • Account identity shared across platforms. Keep individual Plus entitlements separate from venue and organization roles.
  • Trusted server-side verification of payment events, idempotent processing, reconciliation and defined states for trial, active, grace period, expired, refunded and revoked access.
  • Server-enforced paid limits. A client flag or checkout success page never grants authority.
  • Restore purchases, manage subscription, prevent duplicate cross-platform subscriptions and preserve free features after cancellation.
  • Test renewal, failed payment, refund, account switch, device change and delayed webhook cases. Real native purchase testing requires an appropriate development/store sandbox build, not Expo Go previews. Expo guidance.
  • Approve dependencies, service integrations and exact database changes before implementation. No billing library, migration or production configuration was added for this report.

The useful comparison

At these trial prices, one €49 venue contributes roughly the same list-price amount as ten €4.99 consumer subscriptions. This is why venue willingness to pay deserves an early experiment.

Compare contribution after host onboarding, support and selling costs as well. A higher price does not automatically mean a better business.

Budget for the actual service

Track payment and store fees, taxes, database and Realtime traffic, media storage, translation, moderation, customer support and acquisition. Cost limits should follow usage evidence.

Measure cohort retention and contribution margin before using lifetime-value estimates or scaling paid acquisition.

Earn the next phase.

Milestone gates are proposed decision rules, not industry benchmarks or delivery estimates.

Make a safe, useful room

Specify and implement reporting, blocking and moderation. Validate auth, joining, notification preferences and first-conversation flow. Define audience and community rules.

Exit: the owner verifies abuse handling and the core flow on web, iOS and Android before a public pilot.

Run a concentrated pilot

Recruit 5-10 hosts in one segment. Run at least four weekly event cycles. Interview people who join but never speak and hosts whose rooms remain quiet.

Exit: repeated, unprompted participation appears across several gatherings. Choose activation and return thresholds before the pilot, then inspect cohorts rather than pooled signups.

Ask venues to pay

Offer a clear €49/month pilot package and compare it with a €39 event pass for occasional hosts. Deliver a useful host report and show the next recurring event.

Exit: seek at least three actual paying hosts and evidence of second-payment renewal. Treat this as an early signal, not statistical proof.

Test one consumer upgrade

Show repeat participants a concrete Plus benefit. Test one price or package change at a time with a holdout that retains the free experience.

Exit: paid retention improves without harming free-user conversation, connection rates or trust. Add more tiers only if distinct demand emerges.

Resolve the business choices first.

Pick the first audience

Recurring local communities are the recommended starting point. Tourist and nightlife segments have different return patterns, moderation needs and reasons to pay.

Confirm the market

Choose the first city, minimum age, business entity country and initial storefronts. These determine provider onboarding, pricing presentation and applicable checkout programs.

Protect the product promise

Decide separately whether QR Chat should stay temporary or add persistent communities. Do not make retention policy an accidental side effect of a paid tier.
